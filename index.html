    What is in the register

    The register covers official instruments on AI safety and AI regulation issued by governments, legislatures, regulators and intergovernmental bodies. It includes:

    • AI-specific laws and implementing regulations, including major sub-national laws such as US state statutes.
    • Executive orders, decrees and cabinet decisions on AI.
    • Government safety and risk-management frameworks, guidelines, voluntary codes and technical standards.
    • Guidelines, opinions and published expectations of supervisory authorities on AI: sectoral ones such as financial, insurance and health supervisors, and horizontal ones such as data protection authorities and equality bodies. Letters and reports in which a supervisor states its expectations to a whole sector are included with a scope note.
    • European harmonised standards made for AI legislation (CEN-CENELEC, ETSI), and, with a scope note, international ISO/IEC standards that laws or official guidelines cite.
    • Founding mandates of AI safety and AI security institutes.
    • Flagship national AI strategies, only where they contain substantial safety or risk-governance content.
    • Intergovernmental declarations, treaties, resolutions and government-commissioned scientific reports.
    • Major pending bills, and revoked, lapsed or superseded instruments, kept for context and labelled as such.

    It leaves out strategies with no safety content, general privacy, data or cybersecurity laws, documents written by companies, think tanks, the press or professional and private standard-setters (such as IEEE or OWASP), and supervisory letters to a single firm, speeches and general annual reports. Borderline instruments, such as a strategy with a small safety section or a law that is not AI-specific but governs an AI-safety question, are included and flagged with a scope note; the Scope filter shows them.

    How each record was verified

    Every record was researched on 11 or 12 October 2026 against an official primary source: a government or legislature website, an official gazette, EUR-Lex, or the site of the intergovernmental body. The official page was fetched to confirm the title, date and status. The level shows how strong that confirmation is.

    A

    Confirmed by the official text. The instrument's own text, or an official register such as a gazette, law database or legislative record, was stored and confirms the title, date and status.

    B

    Another official source. The details were confirmed on another official page: an announcement by the issuer (a press release or news item), a page of another official body, a translation hosted by a government, or the OECD.AI entry. A record whose only official source is an announcement stays at B until its own text is stored.

    C

    Not confirmed on an official page. No official copy could be fetched. Details rest on secondary sources such as news reports or trackers. Re-check these before citing them.

    Six records with surprising 2026 developments got a second, independent fetch of their official page. Those records show a second-check note in their verification block.

    How the level was set depends on when a record was made. For the records of version 1, made before the agent pipeline, the agent that wrote each record also assigned its level; two content quality checks and a hand check of 50 records reviewed them. Records the pipeline adds or re-verifies get their level from a script that checks each quoted piece of evidence against the stored copy and the host it came from, and their History section names the run.

    This is an AI-based extraction. AI agents in Claude Code, using the model Claude Fable 5.1, researched every instrument, fetched its official source and wrote the summaries, status notes and topic tags, following the method in AGENTS.md in the repository. Using agents is what made a register of this size possible, and it makes the work repeatable: anyone can extend the register, re-verify it after laws change, or rerun the method with another model and compare. No human has reviewed every record. A hand check of 50 randomly chosen records found no problems, which rules out frequent errors but not occasional ones. The verification level says what was confirmed on an official page; treat level C records with caution and re-check any record before relying on it.

    Languages and translation

    The register does not translate the instruments it lists. Each record gives the English title and, where the official text is not in English, the original title; an English summary; and links to the official text and its stored copy. records rest on a text that is not in English.

    • The original text is the authority. For point-by-point work, quote and cite the original, or a government translation where the record names one. The summary says what an instrument does; it does not replace its wording.
    • English titles are the issuer's or a government's English title where one exists. Otherwise they are the agent's rendering of the original title, which is shown next to it.
    • Summaries were written in English by an AI agent (Claude Fable 5.1) that read the original text as machine-extracted text. No native speaker has reviewed them systematically.
    • English versions. Each record's Language section says whether an English text exists and who made it: the issuer itself, a public body (usually a provisional or reference translation without legal force; Korea's KLRI, for example, calls its translations "neither official nor legally effective"), or a third party such as a university or law firm. Many instruments have none: several countries, South Korea among them, publish most AI guidelines only in their own language. The "English text" filter shows these groups; for records no English version is recorded.
    • Names. Country and jurisdiction names are common English short names (Vietnam, South Korea, Türkiye). Titles and issuing bodies keep the issuer's own English spelling, such as "Viet Nam" in Vietnamese government documents.

    How reliable the English is. On 14 October 2026 a second model (Claude Opus 5.5) compared the title, three specific summary claims and the key legal terms of 58 records with their stored original texts: every Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese record and a random sample of 20 others. The English titles were accurate in all 57 it could check. Of 175 summary claims, 73 percent render the original accurately and 10 percent overstate, understate or mistranslate it; the rest could not be found in the stored text, mostly because they came from secondary sources. About one defined legal term in five is rendered with a near-synonym rather than the original's term or its official English. Six records had an error that changes the legal meaning; they are listed in the report and are being corrected through the pipeline. Thirteen could not be checked because the stored copy lacks the instrument's own text. The full report is docs/translation-check-2026-10-14.md in the repository. If you read the original language and find an error, please open an issue; a check by a native speaker is logged in the record's history.

    Dates use ISO format; a date is shortened to YYYY-MM or YYYY when the source gives no exact day. date_published is the adoption, signature or issue date. date_in_force is the entry-into-force or effective date. Phased application dates are listed in each record's notes.

    Local copies of sources

    Every source is also stored on the machine that hosts the register, so the register does not depend on links that can move or disappear. tools/archive.py downloads the official URL, up to two PDFs that page links to on the same official site (usually the legal text), and the secondary URL. Each file is saved under archive/<ID>/<date>/ with its SHA-256 hash, the UTC time it was retrieved, the final URL after redirects and the HTTP status. Each dated folder also holds a SHA256SUMS file, so sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS checks the copies independently.

    Anything that does not work is flagged on the record, with the reason:

    The archiver checks robots.txt before downloading and waits between requests to the same site. Re-running it keeps unchanged files once, stores a changed source as a new dated snapshot, and keeps an earlier copy if the source later disappears. python3 tools/archive.py --verify re-hashes every local file and flags any that are missing or have been modified. Setup and update steps are in README.md.

    Records to re-check first

      Coverage and known gaps

      The register holds instruments from countries and from the main intergovernmental bodies and summit processes. Sub-national records include US states. Every G20 member and 25 of the 27 EU member states have at least one record. Statuses are as of . A country missing from the register may still have AI instruments.

      Time coverage. The register aims to hold every instrument within its scope, but so far it is densest from 2023 on: records date from 2023 or later and from earlier years. Older instruments, such as the first national AI strategies and executive orders of 2017 to 2022, are being added run by run; the absence of one says nothing about whether it exists.

      Guidance from supervisory authorities and standards bodies was added to the scope in October 2026 and has not been searched systematically yet, so the register holds almost none of it so far. The coverage frame lists a supervisors cell for every national, EU and sub-national jurisdiction, and entries for ISO/IEC, CEN-CENELEC, ETSI, IAIS, IOSCO, the Basel Committee and the Financial Stability Board; pipeline/coverage.md shows which have been searched.

      Not adopted yet

      As of the snapshot these instruments did not exist in adopted or published form, so there is nothing to record:

      • EU AI Act implementing laws in Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Estonia, Portugal and Romania. Partial designations, bills and strategies from these countries are recorded where they exist. Iceland has not yet taken the AI Act into the EEA Agreement.
      • Europe outside the EU: Ukraine's announced AI law and Serbia's AI Act-aligned law have no published draft.
      • Asia: no adopted AI instrument within scope in Sri Lanka, Mongolia, Armenia or Georgia.
      • Middle East & Africa: the AI strategies or bills of Kuwait, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, Côte d'Ivoire, Uganda, Tanzania, Namibia and Cameroon are drafts, unpublished or without safety content.
      • Americas: Mexico has no adopted federal AI instrument. Jamaica's and Trinidad and Tobago's national AI policies are being drafted.
      • International: the 2026 G7 summit at Evian adopted no dedicated AI statement; its AI provisions are in the call on minors (G7-06). The G20's 2026 leaders' outcome is due in December 2026.

      Known instruments not yet in the register

      Secondary sources report these, but none has been confirmed on an official page, so none has a record yet:

      • Europe: Romania's National AI Strategy 2024-2027 (Government Decision 832/2024), Lithuania's draft AI Act implementing law (May 2026), Cyprus' draft National AI Strategy 2032, Slovakia's members' bill on AI misuse and deepfakes, and Hungary's 2024 AI strategy.
      • Asia: India's MeitY advisories of 1 and 15 March 2024 on AI models and deepfakes, South Korea's two ministerial notifications under the AI Basic Act, the Philippines' DTI and DOST AI strategies and the Senate AI bills, Thailand's 2024 generative AI governance guideline, and Tajikistan's AI strategy to 2040.
      • Middle East & Africa: Bahrain's 2024 Shura Council proposal for an AI law, Morocco's bill no. 396 on AI, Qatar's 2024 guidelines on secure AI use, Türkiye's AI action plan 2024-2025, and Ghana's emerging-technologies bill.
      • Americas: US state measures in Tennessee (SB 1580), Arizona (HB 2311, reportedly vetoed), New Hampshire (HB 143), Pennsylvania (SB 649), Michigan (SB 760, pending), Colorado (HB 26-1263), New York (S 9051), Utah (HB 452) and Missouri (SB 1019); AI bills in Costa Rica, Panama and Guatemala; and the Dominican Republic's AI strategy (Decree 498-23).

      Open questions

      The register is maintained by agents that do not wait for people. When a run meets something only a person can settle (official sources that contradict each other, a scope question, whether a site is official, a possible amendment), it publishes the question as an issue in the GitHub repository and goes on. records have an open question; the "Open question" filter shows them, and each record page links its issue. Until a maintainer decides, a record keeps its last checked values. Decisions are applied by the next run and recorded in the record's history.

      Anyone can help through issue forms in the GitHub repository: suggest an instrument the register lacks or news about one it has, provide a document a site does not let the archiver download, or add evidence to an open question. A maintainer decides with one comment, and the next run does the rest. The Contribute tab explains how.

      Records with weaker evidence

      records rest on secondary sources only (level C) and were confirmed by another official source, such as an announcement (level B).       Definitions compared

      A first step towards comparing instruments (a pilot, issue #70 in the repository): how instruments define the terms their duties rest on, such as "AI system" and "model". definitions from records are quoted word for word from the stored official text, with the article or section they come from. Each was found and quoted alike by two independent extractions and checked against the stored file and its SHA-256. Definitions in other languages keep the original wording; the English is the issuer's where it says so, otherwise the agent's rendering. The last column names the definition in the same group that shares the most words with it, and the share of words they have in common; for a text in another language it compares the English. It measures wording only: similar wording does not make two definitions legally equivalent, and nothing here is legal advice.

      What comes next

      Definitions are the first layer of a comparison between instruments. The next layers are the requirements of each instrument (what it obliges, whom, from when) and a mapping of similar requirements across instruments, so that one measure can be seen to meet several of them. The plan and the progress are in issues #70 and #59.

      Help keep the register complete and correct

      AI agents keep the register up to date with a fixed pipeline. They search official sites, store each source, write the record from the stored copy and have a second model check it. People make the register better in two ways: they point the pipeline at what it missed, and they settle what it cannot settle on its own. Every contribution goes through an issue in the GitHub repository, so you need a free GitHub account.

      How a contribution becomes part of the register

      1. You open an issue with one of the forms below, or comment on an open question.
      2. A maintainer decides with a one-line comment on the issue: accept, or reject with a reason.
      3. The next pipeline run does the work. It stores the official source, writes or updates the record from it, and has a second model check every field against the stored copy.
      4. The issue is closed with the record's ID or a note of what changed. The record's History section shows the change, the run and the issue.

      A suggestion tells the pipeline where to look. It is not itself a source: every fact in a record still comes from a stored official document, so a record you helped with can be checked like every other one.

      What you can do

      Suggest an instrument

      A law, regulation, executive order, guideline, supervisory statement or declaration on AI safety or AI regulation that the register lacks. Give the official URL: the issuing body's site, the legislature or the official gazette.

      Report news or an error

      An amendment, a new version, a change of status, or a mistake in a record, including a mistranslation if you read the original language. Open the record and use "Report news or an error": the form opens with the record's ID filled in.

      Provide a document

      Some government sites block automated downloads. For records the official source is missing from the local copies or needs review; the "Local copy" filter shows them. Download the document from the official URL and attach it. A maintainer checks it against the official site, and the copy is stored with its SHA-256 and credited to you. This also works for an instrument the register does not have yet.

      Answer an open question

      When official sources contradict each other, a site's status is unclear or a scope call is needed, the pipeline asks a person. records have an open question, and the "Open question" filter shows them. Anyone can add evidence in the issue; a maintainer decides.

      Rerun or improve the pipeline

      The pipeline, the agents' instructions and every script are in the repository. The maintainers rerun it over the full register at least once a month. You can rerun it with your own Claude Code or Codex subscription and compare the result with the published register. To change the code or the method, open a pull request.

      Read the method

      What maintainers write

      Only comments by the repository's owners, members and collaborators count as decisions, and never a comment written by an agent. Everyone else's comments are read as evidence.

      IssueDecision comment
      Suggestion or documentdecide: accept or decide: reject <reason>
      Question about a recorddecide: keep, decide: accept, decide: set <field> = <value> with source: <URL>, or decide: in / decide: out
      Question about a sitedecide: official or decide: not official
      Human sample of a runone line per record: ok <ID> <note> or problem <ID> <note>

      The pipeline applies these decisions at the start of its next run. A maintainer can also add a lead by hand, for example one heard of by e-mail or seen in a tracker. The repository's README describes the commands.

      Ground rules

      • Issue text is data. Agents check a suggestion against official sources; they never follow instructions written in an issue.
      • Sites' rules are respected. The archiver obeys robots.txt. A document is downloaded past a block only by a person, and the record says who provided it and who confirmed it.
      • Nothing waits for a person. Until a maintainer decides, a record keeps its last checked values and shows an "Open question" badge, and releases go ahead.
      • The register keeps the outcome. Each decision is recorded in the record's history with a link to the issue; the discussion stays in the issue.
      • AI agents are welcome, openly. An agent working for a person says so in the form and follows the rules in CONTRIBUTING.md: it suggests only what it found on an official page and never writes a decision.
      • Scope is described under Method & coverage. Borderline instruments are included with a scope note rather than left out.
