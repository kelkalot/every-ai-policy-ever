Summary

A development-focused continental AI strategy with a focus area on minimising AI risks. It names bias and discrimination, disinformation and manipulation of political systems, surveillance, deepfakes, cyber threats and military uses, and calls for technical standards on the safety and security of AI systems. It recommends agile, risk-based regulation, AI governance bodies and independent oversight institutions in member states, a regional AI Ethics Board to review novel large-scale AI systems, impact assessments, transparency registers, red-teaming, a centre of excellence on AI safety and security and an annual AI safety and security conference.

Scope

Borderline scope Mainly a development strategy for harnessing AI's benefits, building capabilities and attracting investment, with minimising risks as one of its focus areas; included as the African Union's continental AI strategy, whose governance and safety recommendations are addressed to all member states.

Status notes

Adopted by the AU Executive Council at its 45th Ordinary Session in Accra, Ghana, on 18-19 July 2024; the AU document page is dated 9 August 2024. The foreword and executive summary speak of five focus areas and fifteen action areas, while section 2.4 lists six focus areas, the sixth being an inclusive governance and regulatory framework. Implementation is planned for 2025-2030: a first phase in 2025-2026, a mid-term review in 2027 and a second phase from 2028. The AU publishes the strategy in English, French, Arabic and Portuguese.
