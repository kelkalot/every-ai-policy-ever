Summary

Draft national AI policy with a rights-based, risk-tiered governance core. It would categorise AI as prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk or low-risk, with high-risk systems (law enforcement, biometrics, credit, welfare, employment, essential services) subject to data-quality, risk-management, human-oversight and transparency duties. It prohibits AI social scoring, indiscriminate biometric mass surveillance, manipulative systems and AI-driven election interference and deepfakes, mandates Algorithmic Impact Assessments for significant public-sector and private high-risk systems, and proposes an Independent Oversight Committee, liability rules, a sunset on 31 Dec 2030 and an AI Act by 2028.

Status notes

Draft v1.1 was released on 25 Jan 2026 and Draft v2.0 on 9 Feb 2026; online public consultation ran from 26 Jan to 8 Feb 2026, and the policy committee was then reviewing the submissions. It replaces the April 2024 draft National AI Policy of the previous government, which was never adopted. The Independent Oversight Committee is to sit within the executive council of a proposed NDGIA body that would itself require legislation. Drafted under the interim government with a National AI Policy Steering Committee formed in July 2025.
