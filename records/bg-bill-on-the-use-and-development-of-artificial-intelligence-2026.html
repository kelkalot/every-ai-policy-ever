Summary

Private members' bill that would apply Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and govern AI in the public sector, AI skills, investment and AI risks to national security and children. It splits market surveillance among sector regulators, makes the Minister of Innovation and Digital Transformation the notifying authority and the Communications Regulation Commission the single point of contact. It bans remote biometric identification and the AI Act's prohibited practices, prohibits publishing convincing AI fakes of senior officials, or of others without consent, with removal orders, bars training models on children's data save narrow exceptions with parental consent, and sets fines up to BGN 5 million.

Status notes

Filed on 24 September 2026 by ten members of the 52nd National Assembly, led by Bozhidar Bozhanov, and distributed on 25 September 2026 (order 52-650-01-318) to the Committee on Innovation and Digital Transformation as lead committee and to sixteen other committees. By 17 October 2026 no committee report for first reading was listed; opinions had been filed by, among others, the Ministry of Innovation and Digital Transformation and the State Agency for National Security (15 October 2026). The bill would enter into force two months after promulgation, except Article 22, § 7 and § 21, from 1 July 2027. It would also create a national AI coordinator appointed by the Prime Minister, an advisory council at the Council of Ministers and an AI regulatory sandbox, require AI systems that the Council of Ministers designates as high-risk for national security, such as recommender systems of very large online platforms, to give the State Agency for National Security and the ministry their source code, documentation and a description of their training data, and amend the Criminal Code (AI-generated pornographic content), the Election Code (labelling of campaign material made with AI) and the Judiciary Act (courts may not use AI to interpret facts and law).
