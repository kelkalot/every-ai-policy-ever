Summary

Canada's national AI strategy, built on trust, opportunity and sovereignty and organised in six pillars. Its first pillar, protecting Canadians and safeguarding democracy, commits to modernised privacy and online safety laws, legal tools against deepfakes and for safe chatbot interactions, protection of elections from AI-enabled misinformation, CAD 50 million for the Canadian AI Safety Institute to track emerging risks and evaluate AI models, AI transparency work including watermarking, a Canada Trusted AI Certification program, renewed funding for the Standards Council of Canada's AI Program, and work with frontier AI companies against cyber and national security threats from advanced AI.

Scope

Borderline scope Mainly a strategy for AI adoption, skills, sovereign infrastructure and scaling Canadian AI companies; included as Canada's national AI strategy, whose first pillar sets out its commitments on AI safety, online harms, transparency and the Canadian AI Safety Institute.

Status notes

Launched by Prime Minister Mark Carney on 4 June 2026; the strategy page was last modified on 8 June 2026. The Prime Minister's release says that over the next five years the strategy will introduce new legislation, investments and programs. It draws on national consultations in 2025, with more than 11,000 submissions, and a 28-member AI Strategy Task Force. Its legislative commitments concern consumer privacy legislation, online safety laws (including legal tools against deepfakes and for safe chatbot interactions) and the continued review of the Privacy Act. Further measures include applied AI research and testing with law enforcement and security agencies for fraud prevention, cyber defence and threat detection, a National AI Literacy Initiative, and, under the Canada-Germany Sovereign Technology Alliance launched in February 2026, shared AI evaluation standards and benchmarks. The government says it will review and update the plan.
