Summary

Bill passed by the Legislature in August 2024 that would have required developers of the most advanced AI models, those costing over $100 million to train, to guard against critical harms: before training, protect models against unauthorised access and misuse, build in the capability for a full shutdown and adopt a written safety and security protocol, with third-party audits and AI safety incidents reported within 72 hours. It would also have created the Board of Frontier Models, whistleblower protections for employees of frontier AI laboratories and the CalCompute public computing cluster. The Governor vetoed it.

Status notes

Sent to the Governor after the Senate's 29-9 vote announced on 29 Aug 2024, and vetoed on 29 Sep 2024 (date_published is the veto date). The veto message objects that the bill covered only the most expensive and large-scale models, whether or not a system is deployed in high-risk environments, involves critical decision-making or uses sensitive data, warns that smaller, specialised models may prove as dangerous, and asks for an approach informed by an empirical trajectory analysis of AI systems and capabilities. The same day the Governor announced that Fei-Fei Li, Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar and Jennifer Tour Chayes would help lead California's effort to develop guardrails for generative AI; their report followed in June 2025 (US-CA-04). Included for historical context.
