Summary

A binding treaty under which Parties must ensure that activities within the lifecycle of AI systems are consistent with human rights, democracy and the rule of law. It requires measures on transparency and oversight, including identification of AI-generated content, accountability, equality, privacy, reliability and safe innovation; remedies, procedural safeguards and notice of interacting with an AI system; and a graduated framework to identify, assess and mitigate risks, with testing where appropriate and possible bans or moratoria. It applies to public authorities; for private actors, each Party declares how it addresses risks. National defence is excluded.

Status notes

Adopted by the Council of Europe Committee of Ministers on 17 May 2024 and opened for signature in Vilnius on 5 September 2024; the English and French texts are equally authentic. Under Article 30 it enters into force on the first day of the month after a three-month period following the date on which five signatories, at least three of them Council of Europe member States, have consented to be bound. The EU signed on 5 September 2024 and approved the Convention by Council Decision (EU) 2026/1080 of 21 April 2026, after the European Parliament's consent of 11 March 2026; the text was published in the EU Official Journal on 13 May 2026 (OJ L, 2026/1081), and the EU declared that it applies the Convention to private actors through the AI Act. EUR-Lex gives the date of entry into force as unknown (pending notification) or not yet in force. Parties need not apply it to activities protecting their national security interests. A Conference of the Parties follows its implementation, and each Party reports to it within two years of becoming a Party.
