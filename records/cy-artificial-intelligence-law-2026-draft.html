Summary

Draft Cypriot law to apply and supervise the EU AI Act, published for consultation by the Commissioner of Communications on 16 August 2026. The Commissioner, acting through an AI Regulation Directorate, would be notifying authority, market surveillance authority and single point of contact; the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection would supervise high-risk systems in law enforcement, migration and border control, and justice and democracy, with related prohibited practices, and the Central Bank those of financial institutions. It sets serious-incident reporting, investigation powers, fines of up to EUR 35 million or 7% of turnover for prohibited practices, and an AI regulatory sandbox.

Status notes

Posted on the government's e-consultation platform on 16 August 2026, with comments open until 16 September 2026; the platform shows 102 comments and marks the bill as under processing. The draft's long title refers to the application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and its short title is the Artificial Intelligence Law; it would enter into force on publication in the Official Gazette. Other non-compliance carries fines of up to EUR 30,000 (EUR 60,000 for a repeat), and public authorities and bodies fines of up to EUR 50,000 per non-compliance and EUR 50 a day, amounts the draft marks as indicative. The Commissioner could authorise real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement, within the AI Act's limits, by secondary legislation on criteria approved by the Council of Ministers. The Commission's list of single points of contact (last updated 7 September 2026) shows the Commissioner of Communications for Cyprus, marked as a designation still pending final adoption.
