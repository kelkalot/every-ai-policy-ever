Bill amending the Copyright Act (protection of personal characteristics, etc. and changed procedure for review by the Copyright Licence Tribunal)

Summary

Would amend the Copyright Act to make it unlawful to share realistic digitally generated imitations of a person's personal characteristics, such as appearance and voice, or of performing artists' performances, without consent. An exception covers caricature, parody, satire and criticism of power and society, but not imitations that are misinformation able to seriously endanger others' rights or essential interests or to create a concrete danger to life or property. The bill also protects performances that are not artistic works, such as improvisations, and aims to secure the right to one's own body and voice and to get the tech giants to take manipulated content down quickly.

Status notes

Implements the political agreement of 26 June 2025 between the then SVM government (Social Democrats, Venstre and Moderates) and six other parties to create protection against the sharing of digital imitations by amending the Copyright Act. A draft was notified under the EU TRIS procedure as 2025/0654/DK (received 31 October 2025, replacing the withdrawn notification 2025/642/DK; standstill to 3 February 2026, with comments from the Commission). The notified draft, titled in English 'Draft act amending the Copyright Act (Introduction of performance and presentation protection and protection against digitally generated imitations, etc.)', would add a Section 73a (personal characteristics) and a Section 65a (performances and artistic presentations) to the Copyright Act, protect both until 50 years after the year of death of the person imitated or the performer, let courts order infringing copies removed from the medium where they are made available, and enter into force on 1 July 2026; its exception did not yet mention danger to life or property. Culture Minister Zenia Stampe introduced the bill in the Folketing on 8 October 2026 as L 49, under a new title, and hopes it can enter into force at the turn of the year 2026/27. The bill also changes the rules of the Copyright Licence Tribunal (Ophavsretslicensnævnet) so that the same dispute cannot run before the tribunal and the courts in parallel.
