Summary

Draft recommended national standard, proposed by TC260, that sets out an AI security capability maturity model (ASMM) for assessing organisations, also usable by regulators as a reference. It covers data, model and application security in 15 security activities and 39 process areas, such as red-blue adversarial testing, value benchmark testing, backdoor detection, and security operations and emergency intervention. Each process area is assessed on the relevant ones of ten capabilities, among them robustness, explainability, fairness, rights protection and emerging-risk prevention, at five levels from informal execution to continuous optimisation, by self-assessment or a third party.

Status notes

TC260 opened the draft for comment on 26 Aug 2026, with comments due by 25 Oct 2026. The draft has no standard number yet (GB/T XXXXX—XXXX) and leaves its publication and implementation dates blank. Organisations are typed as data-support, model-technology or application-service bodies, and each process area is mandatory, conditionally mandatory or optional for each type; an organisation reaches a level when all its applicable process areas reach it and no unaccepted major security risk remains. At the top level of post-training security, safeguards such as blocking knowledge of biological weapons and cyberattacks must not be removed or significantly weakened when the model runs in agents; at the top level of red-blue adversarial testing, organisations run human uplift trials that measure how far a model raises users' ability to write complex attack code or plan biological harm, and keep the uplift within safety thresholds. Normative references include GB/T 45654-2025 (CN-10), GB 45438-2025 (CN-08) and GB/T 45674-2025 (CN-24).
