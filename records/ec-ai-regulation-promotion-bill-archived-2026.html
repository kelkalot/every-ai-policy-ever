Summary

Unified bill for an organic law regulating and promoting AI in Ecuador, which the National Assembly's education and science committee built from initiatives filed in 2024. With the EU AI Act as one of its main sources, it provided for risk-based classification, prohibited practices, stronger duties for high-risk systems, transparency, labelling, human oversight, registration, impact assessments, sanctions and rules for generative and general-purpose AI, governed by the telecommunications ministry (MINTEL) as lead body, the data protection superintendency (SPDP) as supervisory authority and a National Council for Ethics and Algorithmic Governance. The Plenary archived it on 15 Sep 2026.

Status notes

The committee unified two initiatives: the bill of the same name filed by assembly member Silvia Patricia Núñez Ramos on 20 Jun 2024 (No. 450889), and the Bill for the Promotion and Development of Artificial Intelligence filed by assembly member Karina del Carmen Subía Dávalos on 30 Jul 2024. The Council of Legislative Administration authorised the unification on 6 Dec 2024, and on 16 Dec 2024 the committee chair reported the two bills unified for the first-debate report. On 1 Aug 2025 the committee also took up the Organic Bill on Digital Use and Artificial Intelligence for Children and Adolescents of former assembly member Pierina Correa Delgado; the report does not say that it was merged into the text. On 31 Aug 2026 the committee approved, by seven votes to three, a first-debate report recommending archiving. It found that the text lacked adaptive governance instruments (national guidelines, technical standards, sectoral regulations, codes of conduct, transparency standards, impact assessments for high-risk systems), would raise public spending contrary to Article 135 of the Constitution, risked early over-regulation, classified infringements as minor, serious and very serious but left the ranges and limits of sanctions to the regulation, and did not reflect ISO/IEC 42001:2023 or the Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence, Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law; it called first for strengthening the national AI strategy (EFIA-EC), the AI regulatory sandbox and governance mechanisms. The report's description of the text lists transparency and impact assessments among its contents, while its conclusions list transparency standards and impact assessments for high-risk systems among the instruments it lacks. On 15 Sep 2026 the Plenary, in first debate, approved the archiving motion of the committee chair, Rosa Cecilia Baltazar Yucailla, with 88 votes in favour, 60 against and one abstention of 149 members present.
