Summary

Sets up a controlled testing environment (regulatory sandbox), launched with the European Commission's collaboration, to test how AI systems that may pose risks to safety, health and fundamental rights can meet requirements of the then-proposed EU AI Regulation. Selected providers and users of high-risk systems, general-purpose systems and foundation models implement risk management, data quality, technical documentation, logging, transparency, human oversight, and accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity requirements, self-assess compliance, set up post-market monitoring and report serious incidents. The competent body publishes a report on results, good practices and recommendations.

Status notes

Dated 8 November 2023 and published in BOE No. 268 of 9 November 2023; in force from 10 November 2023, the day after publication. Under its second final provision it was valid for at most 36 months from entry into force, that is to November 2026, or until the EU Regulation laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence became applicable in Spain; Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies generally from 2 August 2026, with earlier dates for some chapters and a later one for the high-risk rules of Article 6(1). The sandbox took the position of the Council of the EU of 25 November 2022 on the proposed Regulation as its reference, and the Secretary of State for Digitalisation and Artificial Intelligence was the competent body. The pilot, whose call was opened by a resolution of the Secretary of State of 20 December 2024, has finished and its results report has been published. The organic law bill on AI that the Government sent to the Congress in 2026 would expressly repeal the decree, given the obligation to set up a new AI regulatory sandbox under the EU Regulation.
