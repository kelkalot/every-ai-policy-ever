Summary

Proposed directive that would have set common rules for non-contractual, fault-based civil claims for damage caused by AI systems. National courts could order providers or users of a high-risk AI system suspected of causing damage to disclose evidence, and would presume non-compliance with a duty of care where a defendant failed to do so. Courts would also presume a causal link between the defendant's fault and the AI system's output under set conditions; for providers of high-risk systems, fault meant breaching AI Act requirements on training data, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, robustness or cybersecurity. Both presumptions were rebuttable.

Status notes

Proposed on 28 September 2022 (COM(2022) 496 final, procedure 2022/0303(COD)). With the revision of the Product Liability Directive it formed a package covering different types of liability: this proposal national claims based on fault, the Product Liability Directive producers' no-fault liability for defective products. Article 5 would have required the Commission to review the directive and to evaluate whether no-fault liability rules and insurance cover are needed for certain AI systems. Annex IV of the Commission's 2025 work programme (COM(2025)45 final of 11 February 2025) listed it for withdrawal, giving the reason 'No foreseeable agreement' and adding that the Commission would assess whether another proposal should be tabled or another type of approach chosen. The Commission approved the withdrawal at its meeting of 16 July 2025, and the withdrawal was published in the Official Journal C series on 6 October 2025 (C/2025/5423); the European Parliament's procedure file records the proposal as withdrawn by the Commission on that date.
