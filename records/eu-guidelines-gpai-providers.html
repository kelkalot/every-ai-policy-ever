Summary

Commission guidelines setting out its interpretation of the AI Act's obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models, which apply from 2 August 2025. A model counts as general-purpose if it was trained with compute exceeding 10^23 floating point operations and can generate language, text-to-image or text-to-video. Only an actor making significant modifications to a model, not minor changes, becomes its provider; the guidelines also set out when providers of free and open-source models are exempt from certain obligations and what adhering to the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice means for enforcement. They are not legally binding but will guide enforcement.

Status notes

Published on 18 July 2025, ahead of the entry into application of the obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models on 2 August 2025. A Commission Communication, available only in English, approved the content of the guidelines, which are available in all 24 official EU languages. Providers of models with systemic risk, which include risks to fundamental rights and safety and risks related to loss of control over the model, must assess and mitigate these risks and notify the AI Office without delay. The Commission's enforcement powers, including fines, apply from 2 August 2026, and providers of models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 must comply by 2 August 2027. The guidelines were developed through a public consultation and reflect input from an expert pool set up by the Commission's Joint Research Centre. The Commission's page for the guidelines shows a last update of 20 April 2026.
