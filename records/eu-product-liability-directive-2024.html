Summary

Replaces the 1985 Product Liability Directive with no-fault liability rules under which software, including AI systems, is a product and integrated digital services are covered. Manufacturers stay liable for defects due to software updates or upgrades, missing safety updates or substantial modifications within their control, and defectiveness is assessed taking into account a product's ability to keep learning after it is placed on the market. Defendants can be required to disclose relevant evidence, and courts presume defectiveness or causation where technical or scientific complexity makes them excessively difficult to prove and the claimant shows they are likely.

Status notes

Adopted on 23 October 2024 and published in the Official Journal (OJ L, 2024/2853) on 18 November 2024; it entered into force on 8 December 2024, the twentieth day after publication. Member States must transpose it by 9 December 2026. It applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 December 2026; Directive 85/374/EEC is repealed with effect from that date but continues to apply to products placed on the market or put into service before it. The Commission's product liability page says the Directive applies to products placed on the market as of 9 December 2026. The Commission is to evaluate the Directive by 9 December 2030 and every five years after.
