Summary

The Commission's first report under Article 112(1) of the AI Act on whether the list of prohibited AI practices in Article 5 and the high-risk use cases in Annex III need to be amended. Drawing on consultation of Member States through the AI Board, a multi-stakeholder consultation and an analysis of AI incident databases, it identifies a potential regulatory gap for AI systems generating child sexual abuse material and non-consensual intimate content, which Article 5 does not prohibit. It flags other systems for monitoring, among them emotion recognition, AI companions and mental-health chatbots, and political disinformation.

Status notes

Adopted on 20 May 2026 and addressed to the European Parliament and the Council. It does not review the list of areas in Annex III, which Article 112(2) requires by 2 August 2028. The report finds its assessment limited: when it was adopted, the rules on enforcing the prohibitions applied only from 2 August 2026, national competent authorities were still being designated and the Commission's guidelines on the classification of high-risk AI systems were still in preparation. It refers to the Commission's proposal for a Digital Omnibus on AI, which would tie the start of the high-risk rules to the availability of standards and support tools, no later than 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems. The next assessment under Article 112 is due in 2026. A summary of the report by Portugal's Agency for Technological Reform of the State says that the political agreement of 7 May 2026 between the Commission and the European Parliament on the AI Omnibus introduced a specific prohibition of AI systems generating non-consensual intimate content or child sexual abuse material into the AI Act.
