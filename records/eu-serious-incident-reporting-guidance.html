Summary

Draft Commission guidance and a reporting template for the obligation of providers of high-risk AI systems, set out in Article 73 of the AI Act, to report serious incidents to national authorities. The obligation aims to detect risks early, ensure accountability, enable quick action and build public trust in AI. The draft guidance clarifies definitions, offers practical examples and explains how the rules relate to other legal obligations, and the approach seeks alignment with international initiatives such as the OECD AI Incidents Monitor and Common Reporting Framework. Both were published for stakeholder consultation.

Status notes

Published for consultation on 26 September 2025; the consultation, which sought feedback and examples on the interplay with other reporting regimes, closed on 7 November 2025. The consultation page gave August 2026 as the date from which the rules apply; following the political agreement on the AI Omnibus, the Commission states that the application of the high-risk rules was postponed to December 2027 for stand-alone AI systems and to August 2028 for AI embedded in products. Separately, on 4 November 2025 the Commission published a reporting template for serious incidents involving general-purpose AI models with systemic risk, which these providers must report to the AI Office under Article 55 of the AI Act; it supports Commitment 9 of the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice.
