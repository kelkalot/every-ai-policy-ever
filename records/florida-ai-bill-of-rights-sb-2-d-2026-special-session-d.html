Summary

Would have created an 'Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights'. Companion chatbot platforms would have had to bar minors from accounts without parental consent, give consenting parents controls and self-harm alerts, remind minors hourly that the chatbot is not human, and take reasonable measures against material harmful to minors. Bot operators would have had to tell users hourly that they are not talking to a human, AI companies could sell or disclose users' personal information only as deidentified data, governments could not contract for AI with entities tied to foreign countries of concern, and commercial use of AI-generated likenesses would have needed consent.

Status notes

Filed on 24 Apr 2026 and referred to Rules; on 28 Apr 2026 it was withdrawn from Rules and passed by the Senate 37-1. In the House it was referred to the Information Technology Budget & Policy Subcommittee and died there on 29 Apr 2026, as did the linked public-records bill SB 4-D. It carries the same title as CS/SB 482 of the 2026 Regular Session (US-FL-01), which died in House messages on 13 Mar 2026. The bill stated an effective date of 1 Jul 2026. The Department of Legal Affairs would have enforced the chatbot, bot and data provisions as deceptive or unfair trade practices, with civil penalties of up to USD 50,000 per violation and a discretionary 45-day cure period, and minors could have recovered up to USD 10,000 from a chatbot platform. It would also have barred schools from giving students AI instructional tools before grade 6, with exceptions, and required parental notice and an opt-out.
