Summary

Advisory ethics opinion of The Florida Bar on lawyers' use of generative AI. Lawyers may use it but must protect client confidentiality, researching a program's policies on data retention, data sharing and self-learning, and should obtain informed consent before disclosing confidential information to a third-party program. They must verify the accuracy and sufficiency of AI research, set oversight policies as for nonlawyer assistants, not delegate the practice of law to it, and not inflate billed time. Chatbots that communicate with clients or third parties must follow advertising rules and disclose that they are an AI program, not a lawyer or employee.

Scope

Borderline scope An advisory, non-binding opinion of The Florida Bar, which is the professional association of Florida lawyers but also administers their professional conduct rules; included for that regulatory role, as guidance to all Florida Bar members on using generative AI under those rules.

Status notes

The Florida Bar's Board of Governors directed the Board Review Committee on Professional Ethics to issue the opinion. It applies the Rules Regulating The Florida Bar on confidentiality (4-1.6), competence (4-1.1), supervision of nonlawyer assistants (4-5.3), fees (4-1.5) and advertising (subchapter 4-7) to generative AI, and draws on earlier Florida opinions on cloud computing, electronic storage, remote paralegal services and metadata. It notes that confidentiality concerns may be reduced by an in-house generative AI, and that no informed consent is needed when no confidential information is disclosed to a third party. The opinion states that advisory ethics opinions are not binding.
