Summary

Creates s. 836.13, F.S., an offence for sexual deepfakes: willfully and maliciously promoting an altered sexual depiction of an identifiable person without consent, knowing or having reason to know it is altered, is a third-degree felony, and victims have a civil action. An altered sexual depiction is a realistic depiction of an identifiable person, produced by any digital or other modification, with another person's or computer-generated nude body parts or engaged in sexual conduct the person did not engage in; a disclaimer is no defence. The act also extends child pornography to images created or modified to portray an identifiable minor and criminalises theft of sexually explicit images.

Status notes

Passed the Senate 37-0 on 3 Mar 2022 and the House 117-0 on 8 Mar 2022; approved by the Governor on 24 Jun 2022 and effective 1 Oct 2022. Victims of an altered sexual depiction may seek injunctive relief, damages of USD 10,000 or actual damages, whichever is greater, and attorney fees; providers of interactive computer, information or communications services, law enforcement, people reporting unlawful activity and participants in legal proceedings are exempt. Section 836.14 makes theft of a sexually explicit image with intent to promote it, and possessing a stolen image with intent to promote it for financial gain, third-degree felonies, and promoting such an image for financial gain without consent a second-degree felony. The act also amends the definitions of child pornography in ss. 775.0847 and 827.071 to cover images created, altered, adapted or modified by electronic, mechanical or other means to portray an identifiable minor, raises damages for sexual cyberharassment and increases penalties for sexual activities involving animals. Section 836.13 was amended by ch. 2025-99 (CS/CS/HB 757), which added offences for generating, soliciting and possessing altered sexual depictions, by ch. 2025-133 (CS/CS/HB 1161, Brooke's Law), which added platform removal duties, and in 2026 by ch. 2026-14, ch. 2026-18 and ch. 2026-20.
