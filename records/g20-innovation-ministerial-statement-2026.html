Summary

Consensus statement of G20 ministers at the Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, ending 2 September 2026. It presents emerging technologies as drivers of prosperity when 'deployed in a secure and reliable manner', supports sector-specific, risk-based approaches where appropriate and calls on members to preserve national sovereignty in technology governance. Six pillars cover pro-innovation policy, AI in public services, workforce skills, intellectual property policies for AI, 'AI for standards and standards for AI' and supply chains. The Carolina Principles favour secure real-world testing and existing sector-specific regulation, with new rules only for novel issues.

Status notes

The White House lists the African Union, Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, the European Union, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Poland, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Korea, Türkiye and the United Kingdom as present at the two-day ministerial, which the Department of Commerce and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy hosted. The other deliverables were the G20 AI Prosperity Objectives and the AI Prosperity Compact, on technical workforce development through voluntary partnerships. The UK government published the statement on 4 September 2026. It contains no provisions on frontier-model risks or AI safety institutes.
