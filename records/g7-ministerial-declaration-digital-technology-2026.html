Summary

Ministers make secure AI the first of four priorities, recognising risks from design flaws, cybersecurity vulnerabilities and misuse by malicious actors, including in cyber activities and chemical, biological and radiological capabilities. They commend the revised HAIP Reporting Framework, welcome the HAIP Friends Group's March 2026 action plan, and plan discussions with members of the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science and the OECD on comparing AI risk-assessment frameworks. The declaration also covers synthetic-content detection and risks to minors from generative AI and chatbots, including AI-generated child sexual abuse material and deepfakes.

Status notes

Adopted at the meeting of G7 Digital and Technology Ministers in Paris on 29 May 2026 under the French G7 Presidency; the text itself gives the date as 'May 29' without the year. The meeting also adopted a G7 Vision on AI openness opportunities and shared language and a G7 Common Set of Principles defining a safer and more secure digital space for minors, which the G7 Leaders welcomed at Evian in June 2026 (G7-06). The declaration also encourages AI procurement guidelines for the public sector, plans continued expert discussions on synthetic-content detection that may contribute to the French Presidency's open-source Meta-detector, and notes a French-presidency policy paper on agentic AI led by Inria.
