Summary

Ten-year strategy ('Ghana 2035: The AI-Powered Society') with eight pillars: AI education, youth jobs, digital infrastructure, data access and governance, ecosystem coordination, sectoral adoption, applied research and public-sector adoption. It aligns with the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, contains a 'Risks of AI' analysis (bias, cybersecurity, privacy, safety, inequality), and proposes a Responsible AI Authority or National AI Office with a legal mandate for coordination, incident reporting and oversight of AI system qualification, a soft guideline-based regulatory approach, possible mandatory insurance for high-risk AI outputs, and safe-AI guidance for developers.

Scope

Borderline scope Mainly a strategy for AI education, skills, infrastructure, data, research and adoption; included as Ghana's national AI strategy, which analyses AI risks and proposes a Responsible AI Authority, incident reporting, safe-AI guidance for developers and tools to audit and certify AI systems.

Status notes

The document is dated December 2025 on its cover. By 1 April 2026 Cabinet had approved the strategy and USD 250 million for a national AI computing centre. President John Dramani Mahama launched the strategy in Accra on Friday 24 April 2026, when USD 20 million was also announced for its short- and medium-term implementation. At the launch the Minister for Communication said the ministry was working on an Emerging Technologies Bill to provide the legal framework for AI and related technologies. The strategy rests on about 40 expert interviews and public-sector consultation workshops held from March to October 2022, and recommends reducing the implementation timeline from ten to five years with periodic reviews.
