Summary

The OECD's framework for organisations developing, deploying or providing advanced AI systems to report publicly on their alignment with the G7 Hiroshima AI Process International Code of Conduct. Built around the Code's 11 actions, its questionnaire covers risk identification and evaluation, risk management and information security, transparency on advanced AI systems, governance and incident management, content authentication and provenance, and research on AI safety. Participation is voluntary and reports are published in full on OECD.AI.

Status notes

Launched on 7 February 2025 as an outcome of the G7 Hiroshima AI Process, after the G7 Digital and Tech Working Group under the Italian Presidency, following the Trento Declaration, asked the OECD to develop mechanisms for monitoring voluntary adoption of the Code. The OECD counts 25 reports in the first round, analysed in its 2025 report 'How are AI developers managing risks?'; MIC Japan's HAIP site says the first responses, from 19 companies, were published in April 2025. Version 2.0, launched on 28 May 2026 at a Tech7 event in Paris on the margins of the G7 Digital and Tech Ministerial under the French Presidency, distinguishes model developers, application developers and deployers and is simplified for small and medium enterprises; 31 reports under it were published on 2 October 2026. Organisations must be based in an OECD member, a GPAI member or an Adherent to the OECD Recommendation on AI.
