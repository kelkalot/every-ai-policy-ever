Summary

Voluntary code that turns the Guiding Principles into 11 actions for organisations developing advanced AI systems. They call for internal and independent external testing such as red-teaming at several points in the lifecycle, with attention to chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear risks, offensive cyber capabilities, self-replicating models and chain-reaction events; post-deployment monitoring and incident sharing; and public transparency reports. Other actions cover risk management and governance policies, security of model weights and insider threats, content authentication such as watermarking, safety research, international standards and data protection.

Status notes

Welcomed by the G7 Leaders in their Statement on the Hiroshima AI Process of 30 Oct 2023, which calls on organisations developing advanced AI systems to commit to applying the Code and says it will be reviewed and updated as necessary. The Code is part of the Hiroshima AI Process Comprehensive Policy Framework (G7-03). Organisations report on how they apply it through the OECD's HAIP Reporting Framework (OECD-02), launched in February 2025; G7 digital ministers commended a revised Reporting Framework in May 2026 (G7-04). The Seoul Declaration of May 2024 (INT-04) says its signatories continue to focus on supporting the operationalisation of the Code.
