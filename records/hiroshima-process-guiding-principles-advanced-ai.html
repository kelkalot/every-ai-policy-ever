Summary

Eleven risk-based principles for organisations developing advanced AI systems, including foundation models and generative AI. They cover pre- and post-deployment risk identification and mitigation, transparency reporting, information sharing, governance and risk policies, security controls, content provenance, safety research and international standards. The principles build on the OECD AI Principles and are a living document meant to apply while governments develop regulation.

Status notes

Welcomed by the G7 Leaders in their Statement on the Hiroshima AI Process of 30 Oct 2023, together with the Code of Conduct (G7-02); the statement says that both documents will be reviewed and updated as necessary and instructs ministers to develop the Hiroshima AI Process Comprehensive Policy Framework by the end of 2023. That framework (G7-03, Dec 2023) includes the 'Hiroshima Process International Guiding Principles for All AI Actors', which apply the 11 principles to all AI actors and add a 12th, on promoting the trustworthy and responsible use of advanced AI systems.
