Summary

Ireland's law giving effect to the EU AI Act through a distributed model in which existing sectoral regulators act as market surveillance authorities. It establishes Oifig IS na hÉireann (the AI Office of Ireland) as an independent statutory body that coordinates them, acts as single point of contact and gives access to technical expertise. Market surveillance authorities get an enforcement toolkit running from compliance notices to prohibition, seizure, fines and prosecution, with independent adjudication and court oversight, and the Central Bank Act 1942 and the Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2014 are amended so that AI Act sanctions can be imposed.

Status notes

Signed into law on 21 Jul 2026 as Act No. 31 of 2026, with 10 Parts, 139 sections and 4 schedules. S.I. No. 403 of 2026 brought the Act into operation on 31 Jul 2026, except paragraphs (a) and (e) of the definition of 'applicable market surveillance authority' in section 78, which name Coimisiún na Meán and the Data Protection Commission for the adjudication and administrative fines scheme of Part 6. S.I. No. 404 of 2026 appointed 31 Jul 2026 as the establishment day of Oifig IS na hÉireann, while the Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment's EU AI Act page gives 2 Aug 2026 as the date the AI Office was established. Paul Byrne, previously of the Medical Council, is the Office's first CEO, and its board has held its inaugural meeting. The government said further legislation, including amendments arising from the Digital Omnibus on AI, would be brought forward in autumn 2026. Nine public authorities identified under Article 77 of the AI Act have powers to access AI Act documentation from 2 Aug 2026; they are not competent authorities under the AI Act.
