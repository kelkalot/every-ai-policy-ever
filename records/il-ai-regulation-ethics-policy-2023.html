Summary

Israel's first government policy on AI regulation and ethics. It favours sector-specific, risk-based soft regulation and international interoperability, names seven challenges (discrimination, human oversight, explainability, disclosure of AI interactions, safety, accountability, privacy), adopts OECD-based ethical principles, and recommends an AI Policy Coordination Center and responsible-AI tools such as a risk-management framework.

Status notes

Published in December 2023 by the Ministry of Innovation, Science and Technology, prepared with the Ministry of Justice's Office of Legal Counsel and Legislative Affairs (Economic Law Department) under Government Resolution 212 of 1 August 2021; a White Paper of 17 November 2022 was its first draft and the basis for public consultations. The policy was published in Hebrew; the ministry's English document summarises it, mainly its recommendations chapter. It gives guidelines and instructions to sectoral regulators on AI in the private sector across the AI lifecycle; the government's policy on public-sector AI is developed separately. The policy also anticipates future work on foundation models and frontier AI.
