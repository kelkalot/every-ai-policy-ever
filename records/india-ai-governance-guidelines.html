Summary

India's national AI governance framework. It sets seven guiding principles or sutras (trust, people first, innovation over restraint, fairness and equity, accountability, understandable by design, and safety, resilience and sustainability) and recommendations across six pillars, including an India-specific risk assessment framework based on evidence of harm, a graded liability system, voluntary measures backed by techno-legal solutions, a national AI incident database, and new bodies: an AI Governance Group, a Technology & Policy Expert Committee and the AI Safety Institute. It finds that existing laws can address many risks and that a separate AI law is not needed at this stage.

Status notes

A drafting committee constituted by MeitY in July 2025 and chaired by Prof. Balaraman Ravindran (IIT Madras) wrote the guidelines. It built on a draft report by a sub-committee on AI governance under the Principal Scientific Adviser's advisory group, which MeitY published for public feedback and which drew more than 2,500 submissions; that draft is superseded by these guidelines. The seven sutras are adapted from the Reserve Bank of India's FREE-AI Committee report. Principal Scientific Adviser Prof. Ajay Kumar Sood unveiled the guidelines on 5 Nov 2025, calling 'Do No Harm' the guiding principle of the framework, ahead of the India-AI Impact Summit (New Delhi, 19-20 Feb 2026). PIB published an explainer on 15 Feb 2026 for the Summit. India has no AI-specific statute.
