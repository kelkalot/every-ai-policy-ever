Summary

Binding amendment to India's intermediary rules on 'synthetically generated information' (SGI): content artificially or algorithmically created or altered so that it appears real and depicts a person or event as indistinguishable from reality. Intermediaries offering SGI tools must use technical measures to block unlawful SGI, such as child sexual abuse material, non-consensual intimate imagery or deceptive depictions of real people or events, and must prominently label other SGI and embed permanent metadata or a unique identifier where feasible. Significant social media intermediaries must obtain and verify user declarations of SGI and label it.

Status notes

G.S.R. 120(E) of 10 February 2026 amends the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 (G.S.R. 139(E) of 25 February 2021); the amendment came into force on 20 February 2026. Besides the SGI provisions, it clarifies that removing or disabling access to information, including SGI, in compliance with the rules does not breach the safe-harbour conditions of section 79(2) of the IT Act; requires intermediaries to inform users at least every three months of the consequences of non-compliance, and those offering SGI tools to warn users of penalties for misuse; and shortens deadlines: removal on a court order or a government authority's reasoned intimation from 36 to 3 hours (rule 3(1)(d) as substituted by G.S.R. 775(E) of 22 October 2025, in force from 15 November 2025), removal of intimate or impersonating imagery on complaint from 24 to 2 hours, grievance resolution from 15 to 7 days, and certain removal requests from 72 to 36 hours. A draft of the SGI amendment was published for consultation on 22 October 2025. The rules follow MeitY's non-binding AI advisories to intermediaries of 1 March 2024, revised on 15 March 2024 to drop a prior-approval requirement for under-tested models. On 30 March 2026 MeitY released a further draft amendment to the IT Rules, open for comment until 16 April 2026, under which intermediaries would have to comply with MeitY's clarifications, advisories, orders, directions, standard operating procedures and guidelines on implementing the rules as part of their due diligence.
