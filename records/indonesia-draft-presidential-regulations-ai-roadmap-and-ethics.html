Draft Presidential Regulations on the National Artificial Intelligence Roadmap 2026-2029 and on Artificial Intelligence Ethics

Summary

Two Presidential Regulations that the Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs is drafting: one on the National AI Roadmap 2026-2029 and one on AI ethics. The roadmap, prepared through a White Paper, public consultation and cross-sector discussion and placed in the programme for drafting Presidential Regulations by Presidential Decree 38/2025; an inter-ministerial committee agreed its four policy directions, including mitigation of the risks of AI use. The ethics regulation is to supply the substantive norms for ethical and responsible AI, through AI safety and security measures against technical, social, economic, cultural and political risks.

Status notes

Presidential Decree 38/2025 of 22 December 2025 places both drafts, with the Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs as initiator, in the programme for drafting Presidential Regulations in 2026. It lists the roadmap for 2026-2030 and gives the ethics regulation's subject matter as AI ethics values, the benefits of applying them, identification and mitigation of risks, safeguards, the roles and responsibilities of the parties, and monitoring and evaluation. On 11 February 2026 an inter-ministerial committee of more than 30 ministries and agencies, chaired by the Director General of Digital Ecosystem, discussed the roadmap regulation and agreed four policy directions: stronger stakeholder involvement, innovation, technology and research capability, and mitigation of the risks of AI use; Komdigi's legal bureau stressed consistency of the policy period agreed up to 2029, and the government targeted enactment of the roadmap regulation in the first quarter of 2026. A plenary of the inter-ministerial committee on the ethics regulation followed on 12 February 2026, with ministries and agencies including the national police, the research agency BRIN, the health ministry and the state intelligence agency. Both drafts went through public consultation, focus group discussions and harmonisation in March 2026; inter-ministerial discussion with the State Secretariat ended on 5 May 2026, after which both were to be submitted to the President for enactment. Komdigi's legal bureau likens the ethics regulation to substantive law and the roadmap to the procedural framework, and says the ethics regulation answers the lack of a national, systematic AI ethics guideline. The AI regulation is to carry no sanctions of its own. The roadmap is to adjust the National AI Strategy 2020-2045 (Stranas KA) after the rise of generative AI. The ministry describes its 2023 ethics circular (ID-01) as interim guidance that does not replace binding regulation.
