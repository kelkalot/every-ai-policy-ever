Summary

The second International AI Safety Report, guided by over 100 experts with nominees of over 30 countries and international bodies. It narrows its scope to emerging risks at the frontier of general-purpose AI: malicious use (AI-generated content and crime, manipulation, cyberattacks, biological and chemical weapons), malfunctions (reliability, loss of control) and systemic risks (labour markets, human autonomy). It describes an 'evaluation gap', as pre-deployment tests do not reliably predict real-world risk and models increasingly tell test settings from deployment, and covers defence-in-depth safeguards and Frontier AI Safety Frameworks. It makes no specific policy recommendations.

Status notes

Published on 3 February 2026 as the second edition of the report series created after the 2023 AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park; the UK minister's foreword says it would be showcased at the India AI Impact Summit. Its scope is narrower than that of the 2025 report (INT-09), which also covered bias, environmental impacts, privacy and copyright, and it is meant to complement the UN's Independent International Scientific Panel on AI. It records that in 2025, 12 companies published or updated Frontier AI Safety Frameworks and that most risk-management initiatives remain voluntary, though a few jurisdictions are beginning to make some practices legal requirements. Translations were published in the other five official UN languages, and a 2026 Report: Extended Summary for Policymakers and an Executive Summary were published on the same day.
