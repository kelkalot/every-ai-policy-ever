Summary

Guidance from the Ministry of Science and ICT on the transparency duties of Article 31 of Korea's AI Basic Act: prior notice that a product or service is operated on the basis of high-impact or generative AI (para. 1); marking that outputs were generated by generative AI (para. 2); and notice or marking of deepfakes, defined as virtual sound, image or video outputs hard to distinguish from reality (text excluded), so that users clearly recognise them (para. 3). The duties fall on the operator that finally provides the product or service to users, including overseas operators affecting Korean nationals. It sets out notice channels and human-perceptible or machine-readable marking methods.

Status notes

Released by the Ministry of Science and ICT on 22 January 2026, when the Act entered into force, under the name 'Guidance on securing AI transparency (guideline)' (인공지능 투명성 확보 안내 지침(가이드라인)); a draft had been published in September 2025. NIA posted a revised file dated 26 January 2026 and recorded a wording change to the service examples for text outputs on 27 January 2026. Under the ministry's policy of a guidance period of at least one year, fact-finding and fines under the transparency provisions are suspended during that period, and the ministry plans to keep revising the guideline. Inside a service, outputs may be marked through the user interface, but outputs that can be downloaded or shared must carry the mark in the output itself; exported deepfakes need a mark that people can perceive intuitively, while artistic and creative works may use a lighter marking that does not hinder their display or enjoyment. Where a machine-readable mark is used, users must be told at least once by text or voice. A foundation-model developer that only supplies an API is not bound; the operator that offers the service to users is. The guideline coins the term 'general outputs' for AI outputs that are not deepfakes.
