Summary

Guidance on the AI impact assessment of Article 35 of Korea's AI Basic Act, a procedure in which operators providing products or services that use high-impact AI assess in advance their impact on people's fundamental rights. The Act asks operators to endeavour to carry it out and requires the State, local governments and public institutions to give priority consideration to assessed products. The guideline explains the concept and legal basis and the main considerations at each stage (preparation, main assessment, follow-up), encourages voluntary assessments for all AI, and appends a report form, examples of how system functions can infringe rights, support information and FAQs.

Status notes

Dated 2026 on its cover (39 pages); NIA's notice of 22 January 2026, the day the Act entered into force, distributed it with the other AI Basic Act guidelines. The assessment is an obligation of effort, which the guideline recommends rather than requires; details may be set by Presidential Decree (Article 35(3)), and Article 28 of the draft Enforcement Decree lists seven elements an assessment must contain, from identifying affected people and rights to an improvement plan. For products and services already offered before the Act took effect, the guideline recommends that operators decide for themselves whether to carry out an assessment. Results of other assessments, including the EU AI Act's fundamental rights impact assessment (Article 27) and assessments under ISO/IEC 42001 and 42005, may be drawn on. The Korea Information Society Development Institute (KISDI) is named as the dedicated agency supporting assessments. The guideline draws on the EU AI Act, the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice and UNESCO's Ethical Impact Assessment, and is to be updated once international standards are finalised.
