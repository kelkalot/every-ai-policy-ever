An Act to Regulate the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Providing Certain Mental Health Services (LD 2082)

Summary

Bars any person from providing, advertising or offering therapy or psychotherapy services to the public, including through Internet-based artificial intelligence, unless a licensed professional provides them; a violation is an unfair trade practice. Licensed mental-health professionals may use AI only for administrative and supplementary support and keep full responsibility for it; supplementary use requires written notice to the client and the client's consent, and AI may not make independent therapeutic decisions, interact therapeutically with clients or generate treatment plans without the licensee's review. Boards discipline violations and adopt rules; IRB-approved research is exempt.

Status notes

Signed on 13 April 2026 as Public Law 2025, chapter 687, as amended by committee amendment C-A (H-980). Section 1 adds 10 M.R.S. §1500-EE; sections 2 to 8 add the same rules on AI support to licensing laws in Title 32 (§§2113, 2600-G, 3300-J, 3820-A, 6207-D, 7009 and 13870). Under those sections a licensee may not deny services to a client who refuses consent to AI use, a client's waiver is void, clients keep their right to sue for harm attributable to AI use, and the boards' implementing rules are major substantive rules. As a non-emergency law of the Second Regular Session of the 132nd Legislature, which adjourned sine die on 29 April 2026, it took effect on the session's general effective date, 29 July 2026.
