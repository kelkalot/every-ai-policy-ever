Summary

Subsidiary legislation implementing the EU AI Act in Malta. The Malta Digital Innovation Authority (MDIA) is the default market surveillance authority, single point of contact and notifying authority (conformity assessment bodies are assessed by the National Accreditation Board), coordinates with the MFSA and sectoral authorities, publishes the list of fundamental-rights authorities, registers Annex III point 2 high-risk systems and runs the national AI regulatory sandbox with priority for SMEs. Anyone may complain to the MDIA. Infringements carry penalties of up to EUR 350,000 per infringement or 1% of worldwide turnover, plus EUR 12,000 per day, on top of the AI Act's own penalties.

Status notes

Made under article 58 of the Malta Digital Innovation Authority Act and published in Government Gazette Supplement No. 21,519 of 10 October 2025. Regulation 1(3) deferred regulations 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 (fundamental-rights authorities, documentation, language requirements, high-risk registration, sandbox, complaints) to 2 August 2026; the designations and penalty provisions are not deferred. The Minister may designate further market surveillance authorities by order in the Gazette. Penalties on a public authority or body are capped at EUR 50,000 per infringement plus EUR 50 a day; proceedings are time-barred two years after the alleged infringement, and MDIA decisions can be appealed under Part IX of the MDIA Act. Regulation 12 adds the regulations, as S.L. 591.05, to the Second Schedule to the Administrative Justice Act. Legal Notice 227 of 2025, in the same Gazette, makes the Information and Data Protection Commissioner the market surveillance authority for high-risk systems used in law enforcement, biometrics, migration, justice and democratic processes.
