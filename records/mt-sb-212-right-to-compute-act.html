Summary

Protects the private ownership and use of computational resources for lawful purposes: government actions restricting them must be demonstrably necessary and narrowly tailored to a compelling government interest, which includes making AI-controlled critical infrastructure facilities adopt a risk management policy. Under section 2-10-205 the deployer of a critical AI system (one that makes, or is a substantial factor in making, consequential decisions) controlling critical infrastructure facilities must develop a reasonable risk management policy after deployment, considering the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, the ISO/IEC AI standard or another recognised framework.

Status notes

Signed by the Governor on 16 Apr 2025 and effective immediately; chapter number 150 was assigned on 17 Apr 2025. The bill's short title still speaks of 'requiring shutdowns of AI controlled critical infrastructure', but the enacted act's title and codified sections require a risk management policy instead and contain no shutdown mandate. Section 2-10-205 names the standard as 'the ISO/IEC 4200 artificial intelligence standard', and a plan prepared under federal requirements counts as compliance. Besides AI-controlled critical infrastructure, the compelling government interests listed in the definitions include addressing deception and fraud, protecting individuals, especially minors, from knowingly distributed deepfakes and other harmful synthetic content, and abating nuisances created by datacenter infrastructure. Critical AI excludes systems for narrow procedural or preparatory tasks, common software such as antivirus or spreadsheets, and conversational tools subject to an acceptable use policy that prohibits unlawful content. The act does not alter intellectual-property rights and remedies or preempt federal law.
