Summary

Non-binding national guidelines built on four pillars (Fairness, Accountability, Inclusiveness and Integrity, Responsibility) that accompany the National AI Strategy. They apply across sectors and the whole AI lifecycle, including systems from external providers, and set a risk-based governance model with indicative low, medium, high and unacceptable-risk tiers, escalation controls and safeguards, data-protection alignment, transparency, explainability and human oversight, accountability, auditability and record-keeping, procurement and vendor governance, and guidance on generative, foundation and agentic AI. They are to inform sector rules and future legislation where risk justifies it.

Status notes

Cabinet agreed on 13 February 2026 to adopt the national AI strategy, AI for Mauritius, as the national guiding policy framework, supported by the FAIR guideline document, and stated that the guidelines will evolve into AI regulations as the ecosystem matures. The strategy and the guidelines were launched on 9 April 2026 at the J & J Auditorium in Phoenix (the ministry's release of 10 April 2026 says 'launched yesterday'). The guidelines state that they are not a law and create no legal obligations, and they do not apply to AI used for military, defence, national security or counter-terrorism purposes.
