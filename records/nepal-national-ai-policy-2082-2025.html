Summary

Nepal's national AI policy. It commits the government to a risk-management and AI governance framework in line with globally recognised standards, including a framework to classify AI systems by risk and mitigate the risks. Action policies include AI laws to assess risks and ensure responsible and accountable use; standards for data, algorithms and technologies; benchmarking and certification of AI systems for quality, safety and reliability; standards against cybersecurity risks and AI-driven misinformation; a regulatory sandbox; and measures against deepfakes and personal biases. An AI Regulation Council chaired by the ICT minister is to develop standards and regulate AI.

Status notes

The Cabinet endorsed the policy, presented by the Ministry of Communication and Information Technology, at its meeting of 11 August 2025, and the government spokesperson announced the decision on 14 August 2025 (The Himalayan Times, 14 August 2025); the policy text itself is undated. The Nepal Law Commission lists the policy under 6 Asoj 2083 (22 September 2026). The AI Regulation Council is chaired by the Minister for Communication and Information Technology and comprises the secretaries of the Office of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers and of the ICT, finance and education ministries, the chair of the Nepal Telecommunications Authority, the CEO of the E-Governance Board, three experts from the private sector and universities (at least one a woman) and the chief of the National AI Centre as member secretary; it meets at least twice a year, and the National AI Centre serves as its secretariat. The Ministry reviews implementation annually, and the policy is to be reviewed every two years. Section 14 on risk identification concerns risks to the policy's implementation; the classification of AI systems by risk is to come from an AI governance framework under action policy 9.53.
