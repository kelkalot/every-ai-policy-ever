Summary

Declaration of the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi, held after the Bletchley, Seoul and Paris summits. It calls for international cooperation along seven pillars: human capital, access for social empowerment, trustworthiness of AI systems, energy efficiency, AI in science, democratising AI resources, and AI for economic growth and social good. Its secure and trusted AI section calls secure, trustworthy and robust AI foundational to trust and recognises security in AI systems, industry-led voluntary measures, technical solutions and policy frameworks across the AI lifecycle. It notes the voluntary Trusted AI Commons, a platform of technical resources, tools, benchmarks and best practices.

Status notes

The declaration is headed 'AI Impact Summit Declaration, New Delhi (February 18 - 19, 2026)', and its preamble says the participants gathered in New Delhi on 19 February 2026; the European Commission announced on 20 February 2026 that the EU would endorse it, and the Australian government gives the summit as 19 and 20 February 2026, so only the month is given. The Commission calls it the Leaders' Declaration at the AI Impact Summit, the Australian government the India AI Impact Summit Declaration. The endorsement list published with the text names 89 endorsers: 87 countries, among them the USA, China, India, Russia and the UK, plus the EU and IFAD. Under its pillars the declaration takes note of voluntary deliverables, among them the Charter for the Democratic Diffusion of AI, the Global AI Impact Commons, the Trusted AI Commons and its guidance note, the International Network of AI for Science Institutions and the Voluntary Guiding Principles on Resilient, Innovative, and Efficient Artificial Intelligence.
