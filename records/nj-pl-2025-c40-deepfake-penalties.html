Summary

Defines 'deceptive audio or visual media' as recordings, images or other representations that appear to a reasonable person to realistically depict speech, conduct or writing of a person who did not engage in it, produced substantially by technical means. Generating such media, or soliciting, disclosing or using it, to attempt or further any crime or offence (such as harassment or endangering children) is a third-degree crime; knowingly or recklessly disclosing media so generated is a fourth-degree crime; fines reach USD 30,000. Victims can sue for actual damages of at least USD 1,000 per knowing or reckless violation, punitive damages, attorney's fees and equitable relief.

Status notes

Introduced on 5 Feb 2024 as A3540, for which the Senate bill S2544 was substituted. The Governor conditionally vetoed the bill on 17 Mar 2025; the Assembly adopted his recommended amendments, which among other things made knowing or reckless disclosure a fourth-degree instead of a third-degree crime, and both houses passed it on 24 Mar 2025. Approved on 2 Apr 2025 and effective immediately. The law does not apply to content that a reasonable viewer or listener would understand as criticism, comment, satire, parody, news reporting, teaching, scholarship or research, nor to interactive computer service providers, cloud services providers or commercial AI developers not deemed publishers under 47 U.S.C. 230. Disclosure to law enforcement, to a school administrator or other advocate to report a violation, or for investigations of fraud, cybersecurity, identity theft or harassment is exempt. The Governor's office said the law was inspired partly by the advocacy of a Westfield High School student targeted by peers who created and shared AI-generated explicit images.
