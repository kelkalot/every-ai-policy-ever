Summary

The first intergovernmental standard on AI, adopted by the OECD Council at Ministerial level: five values-based principles for trustworthy AI and five recommendations to Adherents for national policies and international co-operation. Principle 1.4 says AI systems should be robust, secure and safe throughout their lifecycle, in normal use, foreseeable use or misuse, with mechanisms to override, repair or safely decommission systems that risk undue harm. Other principles call for human agency and oversight, transparency, traceability and a systematic risk-management approach across the AI system lifecycle.

Status notes

Adopted on 22 May 2019 (C(2019)34). On 8 November 2023 the Council revised the definition of 'AI system'; on 3 May 2024 the Council meeting at Ministerial level revised the Recommendation to address misinformation and information integrity in the context of generative AI, uses outside the intended purpose and misuse, and the safe override and decommissioning of AI systems. The Compendium lists no later amendment. The G20 AI Principles, welcomed at the Osaka Summit in June 2019, were drawn from it. The Compendium lists the 38 OECD members, 12 non-members (most recently Cambodia and Croatia, on 15 May 2026) and the European Union as Adherents; the OECD.AI overview page gives 47 adherents.
