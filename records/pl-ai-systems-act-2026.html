Summary

Poland's law implementing the EU AI Act. It creates the Commission for the Development and Security of Artificial Intelligence (KRiBSI) as market surveillance authority and single point of contact: a chair, two deputies and four members nominated by the competition, financial, broadcasting and electronic communications regulators, independent in their functions. The Commission inspects compliance, handles complaints, issues warnings and binding individual opinions, can order an AI system that poses a direct risk to life, health, safety or fundamental rights to be withdrawn from the market or from use, imposes the AI Act's fines and runs a regulatory sandbox, free for SMEs.

Status notes

Published in the Journal of Laws (Dziennik Ustaw) on 27 July 2026 as item 1003, with the signature of President Nawrocki. The Act entered into force 14 days after publication, on 11 August 2026; Article 125(4), on setting up the unit of the digital affairs ministry that serves the Commission, applied from 28 July 2026. Articles 8 to 18 (individual opinions and explanations) and Chapters 3 to 5 (inspections, proceedings, settlements on mitigating sanctions), 8 (administrative fines and criminal provisions) and 9 (amendments to ten other acts) apply from 28 October 2026, three months after publication. The minister responsible for digital affairs (informatisation) is the notifying authority. The Sejm, with the Senate's consent, appoints the Commission's chair within two months of entry into force, and the chair convenes the first meeting within three months. A Social Council for AI of 9 to 15 members advises the Commission. The Act caps state spending on it at PLN 9.30 million in 2026, rising to PLN 29.68 million in 2035.
