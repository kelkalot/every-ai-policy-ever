Summary

Endorsing states set out measures for the responsible development, deployment and use of military AI, including autonomous functions and systems: use must comply with international law, notably international humanitarian law, and be accountable within a responsible human chain of command and control. Ten measures cover adoption by military organisations, legal reviews, senior-level oversight of high-consequence applications, minimising unintended bias, appropriate care, transparent and auditable methods, training against automation bias, well-defined uses, rigorous testing and assurance across the life cycle, and safeguards such as deactivating systems that behave unexpectedly.

Status notes

The text holds ten measures (A to J) for the development, deployment and use of military AI capabilities. Endorsing states undertake to implement the measures, make their commitment public and release information on implementation, support other efforts for responsible and lawful use, continue discussions among themselves, refine the measures or add new ones, and engage the rest of the international community, without prejudice to discussions in other fora. The declaration defines AI as the ability of machines to perform tasks that would otherwise require human intelligence, and autonomy as a spectrum involving a system operating without further human intervention after activation.
