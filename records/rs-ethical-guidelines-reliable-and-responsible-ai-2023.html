Ethical Guidelines for the Development, Application and Use of Reliable and Responsible Artificial Intelligence

Summary

Non-binding guidelines adopted by Government Conclusion on 23 March 2023 as a preventive mechanism for checking that machine-learning systems meet the highest ethical and security standards. The Government recommends that state, provincial and local bodies, public institutions and enterprises, regulators and holders of public powers apply them to the AI systems they set up and use, and other developers and users too. It defines AI systems and high-risk AI systems, lists high-risk areas such as biometrics, critical infrastructure and law enforcement, and sets four principles and seven conditions for reliable and responsible AI, with recommendations and an assessment questionnaire.

Status notes

The four principles are explainability and verifiability, dignity, prohibition of harm and fairness. The seven conditions are agency and oversight; technical reliability and safety; privacy, personal data protection and data governance; transparency; diversity, non-discrimination and equality; social and environmental well-being; and accountability. The guidelines rest on the AI Development Strategy for 2020-2025 (Službeni glasnik RS 96/2019), one of whose five goals is ethical and safe AI, and its Action Plan for 2020-2022 (Službeni glasnik RS 81/2020), and draw on the 2021 UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI and the European Commission's April 2021 AI Act proposal. The competent minister determines how their application is integrated. They do not cover systems prohibited by a law regulating AI, and they record that AI needs to be regulated by law. The text is dated February 2023.
