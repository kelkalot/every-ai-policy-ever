Summary

Slovenia's law implementing the EU AI Act. Notifying authorities are the ministries for the market, infrastructure, health and the information society and the medicines agency. AKOS supervises Annex I Section A products, Annex III points 2, 4, 5(a) and 5(d) and Article 50, and is single point of contact, sandbox operator, keeper of a register of high-risk AI in critical infrastructure and SME entry point; the Information Commissioner supervises the Article 5 prohibitions and Annex III points 1, 3 and 6 to 8; sector supervisors cover credit and insurance. Public bodies must publish data on the AI they use. A National Council for Ethics in AI is created; fines reach EUR 35 million or 7%.

Status notes

Adopted by the National Assembly on 23 October 2025, promulgated on 31 October 2025 and published in Uradni list RS 85/2025 on 6 November 2025; in force on the fifteenth day after publication (Article 45), i.e. 21 November 2025. Misdemeanour fines (Articles 25-35) normally run from EUR 25,000 to 250,000 for legal persons and EUR 100,000 to 450,000 for large companies. Where a breach is particularly serious because of the damage caused, the unlawful gain or intent, the ceiling rises to EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices (Article 26(5)), EUR 15 million or 3% for operator obligations and EUR 7.5 million or 1% for incorrect or misleading information, whichever is higher, or whichever is lower for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises. Public bodies that fail to publish AI system information face EUR 500-5,000 (Article 35). The publication duty (Article 21) does not apply to AI used in law enforcement, defence, national security, migration, asylum and border control. AKOS had to set up the first regulatory sandbox by 2 August 2026 (Article 40). Conformity assessment bodies are assessed by the national accreditation service (Article 5). The national AI strategy of March 2026 (NsUI 2030) treats the act as the legal basis of its 'ethics and regulation' measures.
