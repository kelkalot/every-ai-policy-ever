Summary

During an election period (writ to close of polling), it bans publishing, boosting, sharing or reposting online election advertising that is digitally generated or manipulated (AI deepfakes or conventional edits) and realistically shows a candidate saying or doing something they did not. Returning Officers can issue corrective directions. Individuals face fines up to S$1,000 and/or 12 months' jail; social media services that fail to comply face fines up to S$1 million.

Status notes

Introduced as Bill No. 29/2024, read the first time on 9 Sep 2024; Minister Josephine Teo moved the second reading on 15 Oct 2024, and the second and third readings took place that day. The Act commenced on 22 Jan 2025. It inserts s. 61MA into the Parliamentary Elections Act 1954 and s. 42LA into the Presidential Elections Act 1991, extends the Returning Officer's corrective directions to the new offence and lets candidates request them; a candidate who knowingly makes a false or misleading declaration in a request commits an illegal practice. The ban does not apply to private or domestic communications or to news published by authorised news agencies, and a person who did not know and had no reason to believe that the representation was false has a defence. In the second-reading speech the Minister also announced a Code of Practice under the Protection from Online Falsehoods and Manipulation Act requiring social media services to counter digitally generated or manipulated content at all times, to be issued in 2025.
