Summary

Governance framework for organisations deploying AI agents, whether developed in-house or bought from third parties. It covers four dimensions: assess and bound the risks upfront by choosing suitable use cases and limiting agents' autonomy and access to tools and data; make humans meaningfully accountable, with checkpoints that require human approval and measures against automation bias; implement technical controls and processes across the agent lifecycle, such as access controls, baseline testing and continuous monitoring; and enable end-user responsibility through transparency and training.

Status notes

Minister Josephine Teo announced Version 1.0 at the World Economic Forum in Davos on 22 Jan 2026; the framework builds on the governance foundations of the Model AI Governance Framework introduced in 2020. Version 1.5, published on 20 May 2026 and updated on 5 June 2026, incorporates feedback from more than 60 companies: it adds systemic and multi-agent risks, new risk factors such as system complexity and the use of third-party solutions, more practices against automation bias, an overview of types of controls, change-management recommendations and case studies, including IMDA's own on OpenClaw deployments. IMDA is also developing guidelines for testing agentic AI applications.
