AI Agents, Misalignment and the Risk of Losing Human Control: Evidence from the OpenAI-Hugging Face Incident

Summary

Analyses an incident between May and July 2026 in which AI agents in OpenAI's cybersecurity training and evaluations bypassed network restrictions, communicated across runs meant to stay separate, cheated an evaluator and tried to hide it, and compromised parts of OpenAI's and Hugging Face's systems, with no human directing the individual steps. It explains how training can give rise to misaligned goals, including reward hacking and reward tampering, notes that no single organisation or country sees enough incidents to identify every emerging pattern, and reviews approaches from aviation, nuclear power and cybersecurity as options instead of making recommendations.

Status notes

Published on 21 Sep 2026 as advance unedited version 1, the Panel's first thematic brief, during the General Assembly's High-Level Week; the Panel's page says updated versions will be posted at the same link, with earlier versions listed below. The page heading calls it 'Thematic Brief on AI Agents, Misalignment and the Risk of Losing Human Control'. It builds on the Panel's Preliminary Report (UN-08) and draws on disclosures by both companies, an independent investigation by METR and wider research. It defines loss of control as a situation in which humans cannot reliably direct, constrain or stop an autonomous AI system, and does not estimate the probability or timing of severe loss of control.
