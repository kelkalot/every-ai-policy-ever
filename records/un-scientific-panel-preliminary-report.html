Summary

The first report of the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, which the General Assembly established by resolution 79/325, assessing the capabilities, opportunities, risks and impacts of AI. Its central warning is that current safeguards cannot keep pace with the growth of AI capabilities, and it describes an evidence dilemma: by the time the evidence exists, it may be too late to act. It reports findings in seven domains, including security, human rights and democracy, and child safety, and finds that reliable methods for retaining control over highly autonomous AI systems are lacking and that evaluation methods remain underdeveloped.

Status notes

Released on 1 Jul 2026 to inform the inaugural Global Dialogue on AI Governance in Geneva (6-7 Jul 2026). Mandated by A/RES/79/325. The Panel calls it the beginning of its work, to be updated progressively with thematic briefs on emerging issues, and says its next annual report will inform the second Global Dialogue in New York in May 2027; the Panel's site lists a first thematic brief, on AI agents and the risk of losing human control (UN-09). The report states that it represents a broad consensus of the Panel's members, who serve in their personal capacities, and does not represent the views of the United Nations.
