Summary

UNESCO's global standard-setting instrument on the ethics of AI, which Member States are to apply on a voluntary basis. It says that unwanted harms (safety risks) and vulnerabilities to attack (security risks) should be avoided and addressed throughout the life cycle of AI systems, that an AI system can never replace ultimate human responsibility and accountability, and that, as a rule, life and death decisions should not be ceded to AI systems. AI should not be used for social scoring or mass surveillance. Member States are asked to introduce ethical impact assessment and liability frameworks and to develop standards with measurable, testable levels of safety and transparency.

Status notes

Adopted on 23 Nov 2021 at the 41st session of the General Conference, meeting in Paris from 9 to 24 Nov 2021; UNESCO says its 193 Member States adopted it. The General Conference mandated its preparation in November 2019 by 40 C/Resolution 37. To support implementation, the Recommendation commits UNESCO to develop a readiness assessment methodology and a methodology for ethical impact assessment of AI technologies.
