Summary

White House plan of recommended federal policy actions in three pillars: accelerating AI innovation, building AI infrastructure, and leading in international AI diplomacy and security. Its safety and risk content: CAISI to evaluate frontier models for national-security risks such as cyber and CBRNE, and adversary AI systems for backdoors; a DARPA-led programme on interpretability, control and robustness; federal guidelines for AI evaluations; a DHS-led AI-ISAC and AI in incident-response playbooks; requiring federally funded research institutions to use nucleic acid synthesis providers that screen sequences and customers; and a deepfake forensic guideline.

Status notes

The White House announced the plan's release on 23 July 2025; the document itself is dated July 2025. The plan follows Executive Order 14179 (US-11), which directed the creation of an AI Action Plan. The announcement calls it 'Winning the AI Race: America's AI Action Plan' and counts over 90 federal policy actions. The plan is signed by Michael J. Kratsios (Assistant to the President for Science and Technology), David O. Sacks (Special Advisor for AI and Crypto) and Marco A. Rubio (Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs). It says AI-related federal funding should not go to states with burdensome AI regulations, but that the federal government should not interfere with states' rights to pass prudent laws that are not unduly restrictive to innovation, and it asks agencies to consider a state's AI regulatory climate in funding decisions; EO 14365 of December 2025 (US-20) later directed action against state AI laws. It asks NIST to revise the AI RMF (US-02) to remove references to misinformation, diversity, equity and inclusion, and climate change. CAISI's draft NIST AI 800-2 (US-21) answers the plan's call for evaluation guidelines. The White House fact sheet on EO 14434 of 29 September 2026 (US-26) still cites the plan and its more than 90 federal actions.
