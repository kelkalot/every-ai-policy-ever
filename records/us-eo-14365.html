Summary

Sets a policy of a 'minimally burdensome' national AI framework and acts against state AI laws until one exists. The Attorney General must set up an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge conflicting state AI laws; Commerce must publish an evaluation identifying 'onerous' ones and bar states with them from BEAD non-deployment funds as far as federal law allows; agencies must assess whether to condition discretionary grants. The FCC is to consider a preemptive federal reporting and disclosure standard, and the FTC must say when state laws requiring changes to truthful AI outputs are preempted. A recommended preemption law must spare state child-safety, AI infrastructure and procurement laws.

Status notes

Signed 11 December 2025 and published in the Federal Register on 16 December 2025 (90 FR 58499). The order gave 30 days for the AI Litigation Task Force and 90 days (to about 11 March 2026) for the Commerce evaluation of state AI laws, the BEAD Policy Notice and the FTC policy statement; the FCC proceeding is due within 90 days of the Commerce evaluation. The Attorney General established the Task Force by memorandum of 9 January 2026, chaired by the Attorney General or a designee with the Associate Attorney General as vice chair. The order cites a new Colorado law banning 'algorithmic discrimination' (SB 24-205, US-CO-01); on 24 April 2026 the Department of Justice moved to intervene in xAI's suit against that law, alleging that it violates the Equal Protection Clause. The FTC's proposed policy statement on the suppression of accuracy in AI systems of 1 July 2026 (US-24) says the order directs the Commission to issue it. The White House's legislative recommendations of March 2026 (US-22) set out the preemption that section 8 asks for.
