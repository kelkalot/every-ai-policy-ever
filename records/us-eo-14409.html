Summary

Treasury, the Department of War (through NSA) and DHS (through CISA) must develop a classified benchmarking process for AI models' advanced cyber capabilities, with the NSA Director setting the threshold for 'covered frontier models', and design a voluntary framework under which developers can give the government access to such models for up to 30 days before releasing them to other trusted partners. The order states that this authorises no mandatory licensing, preclearance or permitting of new AI models. It also directs CISA Binding Operational Directives, a Treasury-led voluntary AI cybersecurity clearinghouse, and DOJ prioritisation of prosecutions for AI-enabled hacking.

Status notes

Signed 2 Jun 2026 and published in the Federal Register on 5 Jun 2026 (Vol. 91, No. 108, pp. 34565-34567). Deadlines: Sec. 2(a) to (e) within 30 days (about 2 Jul 2026); Sec. 2(f), on expanding the United States Tech Force cybersecurity hiring and placement pathways, and the Sec. 3 benchmarking process and frontier-model framework within 60 days (about 1 Aug 2026). Under Sec. 3 the Secretary of Commerce is consulted through the Director of NIST; CAISI is not named. Under Sec. 2(c)(iii) CISA is to facilitate access to cybersecurity tools and services, including covered frontier models where appropriate, for agencies, state and local authorities and critical-infrastructure operators. The order revokes no earlier instrument. Participation in the pre-release access framework is voluntary for developers.
