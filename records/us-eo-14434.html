Summary

Directs the executive branch to use 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' in place of 'Artificial Intelligence' and 'AI' in non-statutory documents and communications, defining SI by reference to the statutory AI definition (15 U.S.C. 9401(3)). The Assistant to the President for Science and Technology must propose legislative language for a federal SI definition and conforming amendments within 60 days. The order contains no substantive safety requirements.

Status notes

Signed 29 Sep 2026 and published in the Federal Register on 2 Oct 2026 (Vol. 91, No. 190, pp. 63129-63130). Sec. 2(b) does not require any change to previously issued regulations, presidential actions, contracts, grants or other historical documents. Under Sec. 1 it is the administration's policy that the executive branch will not acknowledge the usage of 'Artificial Intelligence' and 'AI' in any applicable setting. The APST's legislative proposal, due about 28 Nov 2026, is also to assess whether the new definition should modify, expand upon or supersede the statutory definition of AI, and to recommend further executive action. NIST's CAISI page now names the centre the 'Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI)' (US-15), and the White House fact sheet on the order refers to a 'National Super Intelligence Policy Framework'. The White House Accord on Super Intelligence (US-27) is dated the same day as the order.
