Summary

Bipartisan House bill on frontier AI models, trained with more than 10^26 operations. All frontier developers would publish transparency reports on new models and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours. Larger developers, tiered by revenue and AI spending, would also publish frontier AI frameworks, register, undergo annual independent audits and report catastrophic risk; the largest would retain a licensed independent verifier. A new Under Secretary of Commerce for AI Security would administer it, the Commerce Secretary could suspend models posing imminent catastrophic risk, and new state catastrophic-risk transparency, audit and incident-reporting rules would be preempted.

Status notes

Introduced on 23 Jul 2026 by Rep. Jay Obernolte with Reps. Lori Trahan, Scott Franklin, Scott Peters, Erin Houchin and Suhas Subramanyam; the bill text published with the announcement leaves the bill number and the committee blank. The sponsors say it was developed as part of their broader Great American AI Act framework. Large frontier developers have AI-related development expenditures of at least $1B and revenues above $50M; very large ones at least $10B and above $5B, measured over 36 months. Preemption (Sec. 9) is aimed at state frontier-safety statutes such as California SB 53, New York's RAISE Act and Illinois SB 315, and preserves generally applicable laws, use- and deployment-based rules for deployers and users, laws protecting minors, and state procurement and use rules. Civil penalties reach $1M per violation per day, and $10M per violation per day for emergency orders, whose wilful violation is criminal. Rules on minimum framework requirements, licensing of independent verification organisations and modification criteria are due within 180 days of enactment. On 1 Oct 2026 Reps. Obernolte and Trahan said the bill would build on the White House Accord that leading AI companies had signed and make it durable.
