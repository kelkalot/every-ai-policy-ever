Summary

Proposed FTC enforcement policy statement, issued as directed by Executive Order 14365, on how Section 5 of the FTC Act applies to AI systems. Since consumers reasonably expect AI systems to aim at truthful and accurate outputs, the Commission considers that companies steering outputs toward undisclosed objectives, away from those users set or reasonably expect, are likely to deceive consumers, even when they do so to comply with a state law such as Colorado's AI Act; state laws requiring such deception would be impliedly preempted. A disclaimer must be clear, conspicuous and prominent, not buried in terms of service. Hallucinations alone do not raise Section 5 issues.

Status notes

Dated 1 Jul 2026. Section 7 of Executive Order 14365 (11 Dec 2025) directed the Commission to issue a policy statement on how Section 5 of the FTC Act applies to AI models and how state laws requiring alterations to their accurate outputs can conflict with it. The Commission voted 2-0 to publish the proposed statement in the Federal Register for public comment, with comments due by 31 Jul 2026, to help it formulate a final policy. The statement takes no position on whether the practices it discusses are also unfair under the FTC Act. It notes that the FTC Act does not expressly preempt state law and relies on implied conflict preemption. It cites Colorado's AI Act, repealed and re-enacted as amended by SB 26-189 (enacted 14 May 2026), as a state law that could lead AI companies to suppress accuracy to avoid liability for discriminatory outcomes. As of 17 Oct 2026 it remained a proposed statement.
