Summary

Federal law that criminalises knowingly publishing non-consensual intimate imagery, including AI-generated 'digital forgeries' (deepfakes), with stronger penalties for content depicting minors. Covered online platforms must set up a notice-and-removal process and take down reported content within 48 hours, enforced by the FTC.

Scope

Borderline scope Not an AI-specific law: it covers authentic intimate images as well as AI-generated digital forgeries. Included as the operative US federal instrument against publishing sexual deepfakes and for their removal by online platforms.

Status notes

Approved 19 May 2025 as Public Law 119-12 (S. 146); the Senate passed it on 13 Feb 2025 and the House on 28 Apr 2025. The criminal offences in section 2 have no delayed start date. Covered platforms had to establish the notice-and-removal process within one year of enactment, so the platform duties apply from 19 May 2026. A failure to reasonably comply with the notice-and-takedown obligations is treated as an unfair or deceptive act or practice under the Federal Trade Commission Act, and the FTC enforces the section also against non-profit organisations.
