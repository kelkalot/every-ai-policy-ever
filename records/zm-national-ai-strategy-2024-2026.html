Summary

National AI strategy of the Ministry of Technology and Science, developed with support from the Tony Blair Institute and Finland and launched in Lusaka on 21 November 2024. Its principles include a human-centric approach, transparency and explainability, accountability, fairness and non-discrimination, data privacy and security, inclusivity and sustainability, aligned with the AU Continental AI Strategy, UNESCO's Recommendation and the OECD AI Principles. Governance measures include a National AI Council, sectoral technical working groups, national AI ethics guidelines, extending the regulator ZICTA's mandate to AI regulation and ethical compliance, regulatory sandboxes and risk management.

Scope

Borderline scope Mainly a strategy for AI skills, infrastructure, research and sectoral adoption; included as Zambia's national AI strategy, whose governance measures include a National AI Council, AI ethics guidelines, extending the regulator ZICTA's mandate to AI and risk management.

Status notes

The cover reads 'National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2024-2026' and the acknowledgement supports implementation of the 'National AI Strategy 2024 - 2026', but the running page header reads '2025 - 2027'. The ministry's release dated 21 November 2024 (published 25 November 2024) reports the launch that day in Lusaka by the Minister of Technology and Science, Felix Mutati. The foreword also commits to a National AI Taskforce and a Centre of Excellence hosting industry AI working groups and innovation labs, and to guardrails for the safe use of AI.
